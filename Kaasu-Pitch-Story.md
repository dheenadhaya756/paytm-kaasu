# Paytm Kaasu: how to tell the story

## The idea in one line

Payments are solved. Money isn't. Every day 1.64 crore Soundboxes say "money received", but none of them tells the shopkeeper what's coming.

## Why this story wins hearts

Judges remember a person, not a feature list. The story follows one rule: **one man, one date, one number.**

- **One man:** Murugan.
- **One date:** 6 January.
- **One number:** ₹32,520.

Everything else, including the features, research and business model, is proof that Murugan's story ends well.

The story moves through four feelings:

1. **Recognition.** "I know a Murugan." Everyone has a neighbourhood kirana.
2. **Worry.** The truck comes on 6 January and he's ₹32,520 short.
3. **Relief.** His own box tells him in November, and he fixes it ₹600 a day.
4. **Pride.** "Pongal stock paid. Happy Pongal, Murugan."

Come back at the end to the first sound you played. That callback is what makes it feel like a story rather than a pitch.

## The 3-minute script

Slide numbers match http://localhost:3000/deck/. Lines in *italics* are actions. Pause where you see "(pause)".

**Slide 1: title, about 10 seconds**
*Press the Soundbox OK button in the live app so it says "money received", or play the chime.*

"That sound plays 1.64 crore times a day across India. It tells a shopkeeper what just came in. (pause) It never tells him what's coming."

**Slide 2: you, about 15 seconds**

"I'm Dheena. I'm an engineer with 8 patents, and today I design fintech at Rock Paper Scissors for teams like Bajaj Finance, Nuvama and Turtlemint. I've built for banks. This time I wanted to build for the man behind the counter."

**Slide 3: Murugan, about 25 seconds**

"This is Murugan. Murugan Stores, Velachery, two kilometres from here. He sells eleven thousand rupees a day."
*Say the Tamil line yourself, slowly:* "வியாபாரம் நல்லா இருக்கு. ஆனா தேவைப்படும் நாள்ல பணம் இல்ல."
"Business is good. But the money isn't there on the day he needs it. (pause) On 6 January his Pongal stock costs eighty thousand. At today's pace he'll have forty-seven. He's thirty-two thousand short, and he doesn't know it yet."

**Slide 4: why it matters, about 20 seconds**

"Murugan isn't careless. He's normal. Half of India's kiranas saw sales fall. Only fourteen percent of small businesses ever reach formal credit. So every festival, they borrow in a panic, at the worst price. (pause) Payments are solved. Money isn't."

**Slides 5 and 6: Kaasu, about 40 seconds**

"So we built Kaasu. In Tamil, kaasu means money. It lives on the Soundbox he already has."
*Switch to the live app. Press "Play brief". The Soundbox speaks in Tamil.*
"Fifty-three days early, his box tells him: save six hundred a day, collect what customers owe, borrow only the gap. He says 'aam', yes. He confirms with his PIN. Money never moves on voice alone."
"His loan goes from thirty-five thousand in a panic to eleven thousand, planned. When his regular customers pre-book their Pongal hampers, the loan can drop to zero."
"And udhaar reminders come from Paytm, not from him. In a small shop, asking your neighbour for money can cost you the customer. Now he never has to ask."

**Slide 7: architecture, about 15 seconds**

"No new hardware and no new app. Kaasu runs on Paytm's Soundbox fleet and UPI. Regulated banks and lenders hold the money and lend, built to RBI's digital lending rules. Maths decides every rupee. AI only explains it, in Tamil."

**Slide 8: business model, about 15 seconds**

"For Paytm it's three engines: a ₹49 subscription, safer festival loans, and new Postpaid customers. At ten lakh shops that's about a hundred and thirty crore a year, on devices Paytm already owns."

**Slides 9 to 11: research, about 15 seconds**

"We checked twelve earlier Paytm hackathon entries and forty-five products. Everyone answers questions. Nobody else answers, acts, brings money in, and plans ahead. That's the gap we built for."

**Slide 12: close, about 20 seconds**
*Slow down here.*

"So here's 6 January. Five in the morning. The distributor's truck stops outside Murugan Stores. And for the first time, Murugan isn't worried. He pays in full."
*Play the chime again.*
"And his Soundbox says: 'Pongal stock paid. Happy Pongal, Murugan.' (pause)
Let's pilot Kaasu in Chennai this Pongal."
*Stop. Don't add anything. Let the silence land.*

## Delivery tips

- **Open with sound, not a slide.** The Soundbox chime is your hook. Use it again at the end.
- **Say the Tamil yourself.** You're in Chennai, so a Tamil line from you lands harder than any recording.
- **Use "Murugan", never "the user" or "the merchant".**
- **Pause after the three numbers:** ₹80,000, ₹47,480 and ₹32,520. Silence makes numbers feel heavy.
- **Show, then tell.** Press the button in the live demo before you explain it.
- **Keep the honesty lines.** Saying "built to RBI's rules" and "these revenue numbers are our assumptions" earns trust from fintech judges.
- **End on the ask and stop.** The last words they hear should be "this Pongal".

## If judges ask

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| "Isn't this a loan app?" | No. Own money first, the loan is only the gap. In the demo the loan shrinks from ₹35,000 to ₹11,000, or to zero. |
| "Is it RBI-compliant?" | Paytm acts as the lending service provider. Regulated partners hold the money and lend. The loan comes with a Key Fact Statement and consent. Money goes directly to and from the shop's bank and the lender. Pongal pre-booking settles to the shop the next day, never held in escrow. Every payment needs the UPI PIN. |
| "Does it already exist?" | Pieces exist. PhonePe Khata shows udhaar to customers, and Paytm Postpaid pays shops. Nobody plans a shop's festival cash ahead, by voice, on the device already on the counter. |
| "What if a customer doesn't repay udhaar?" | Kaasu sees it, keeps the Pongal plan on track by raising savings or the planned loan, and Paytm reminds the customer once more on payday. |
| "Why would Paytm build it?" | Paytm's CEO has talked about AI agents acting as a small shop's CFO. Kaasu is that CFO, on 1.64 crore devices Paytm already owns. |
